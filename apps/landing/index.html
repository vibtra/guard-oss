FAQs

Questions IT asks first.

About pilots, installs, what Guard keeps, what happens on a block, how fast it decides, and the free edition.

Talk to us
How does a pilot work?

Pick a small group of Macs and a policy to test. For 30 days, free, we run block, allow and recovery with you, and check content rules on your own workflows. To continue, we agree an order form; there is no automatic billing. Otherwise Guard is removed. Email sam@vibtra.app to start.

What needs an install?

One Mac app. It bundles the agent and installs one system extension: a content filter and a transparent proxy. Enrol with a link from the Devices page; the app creates a certificate for that laptop and the key never leaves it. Nothing is installed in the browser or in the AI app. If the agent is down, the extension fails open and traffic goes straight through, unchecked. Guard runs on macOS 14 or later.

What does Guard keep?

Per event: the rule id, the match type, counts and, if your organisation allows it, one redacted snippet of at most 80 characters. Prompts are inspected on the laptop and never stored by Vibtra. Only catalogued AI hosts are decrypted; everything else is tunnelled untouched.

What does IT see?

A web console with Overview, Activity, Rules, Requests, Devices, People and AI services, plus Settings. Activity shows who, which app, which service and which rule. Every page is admin-only, and Settings holds monitor or enforce and the staff notice.

What happens on a block?

The request stops on the laptop before it reaches the vendor. The person sees Guard’s notice with the rule and the next step; the vendor’s app shows its own generic error. Request access goes to the Requests page, and a grant reaches the Mac on its next sync. New orgs start in monitor mode, which records rule decisions without enforcing those rules. Connections Guard cannot inspect are still blocked unless IT allows them under Uninspectable TLS in Settings. Enforce mode always blocks uninspectable connections.

How fast is Guard, and how were the numbers measured?

On a MacBook Pro (M4 Max) in October 2026, with Guard’s eight starter content rules and a 1.3 KB chat prompt. Reading the prompt and deciding took a median 1.2 ms with a tax file number in it (1.5 ms at p99) and 0.8 ms without. End to end through the agent, with interception and the event record, a block came back in a median 6.5 ms (10 ms at p99), and an allowed request took about the same extra time. These are test results with synthetic prompts on one Mac, not a guarantee for every request or network.

Which apps and sites are covered?

Verified live on 22 October 2026: the Claude website and the ChatGPT website in Google Chrome 156, Claude for macOS 3.1623.1, and ChatGPT for macOS 26.1016 with ChatGPT and Codex as one switch. Content rules apply to every catalogued service the agent can inspect; they are checked separately from the switches, and in a pilot we confirm them on your own workflows. Other browsers, uploads, voice and connectors are not covered; a newer client release is re-tested before it is marked verified. Guard runs on macOS only.

Is there a free edition?

Guard launches with a free Community Edition. It covers the Claude website, Claude for macOS, the ChatGPT website and ChatGPT for macOS, and keeps them current: fixes for vendor changes reach it the same day they reach paying customers. There is no limit on Macs, people or usage. New apps and sites, enterprise features such as SCIM directory sync, hosting by Vibtra and support are paid. Email sam@vibtra.app to discuss them.
